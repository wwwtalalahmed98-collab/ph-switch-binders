Pick a design — the geometry is its own

Histidine is the only amino acid whose side chain titrates near physiological pH: free imidazole has a pKa of about 6.0, so it is 3.8% charged at 7.4 and 50% charged at 6.0. Every other residue holds the same charge across that span. That thirteen-fold swing is the entire mechanism, and the reason the design places histidines and nothing else.

What is real here and what is not. Each histidine's distance to its partner is measured from the predicted complex, and the i_ptm is the re-predicted score of that exact sequence. The charge curve is the Henderson–Hasselbalch equation. The release is the designed mechanism, not a simulation: AlphaFold has no concept of protonation state, so no model used in this project can show the binder letting go. That is what the wet-lab assay is for.

Forty-three tries, twenty survivors

Every design was scored by an independent re-prediction of its own sequence, never by the number the optimiser reported about itself. Each stage below threw work away on purpose.

9.6 GPU-hours on two T4s. The widest cut is the first one: two thirds of generated designs never bind well enough to be worth anything downstream.

Three things that nearly went wrong

0.667

A confident binder aimed at nothing. It scored second best of sixteen and touched 27 residues of TNF-α — none of them in the 25-residue target site. Score alone would have put it in the submission.

68%

Histidines that moved the binder. One design kept just 68% of its epitope where its own histidine-free twin kept 92%. The twin is what made the cause provable. Dropped.

12/14

Redesign that looked like an upgrade. On the first challenge, ProteinMPNN raised fold confidence and destroyed twelve of fourteen interfaces. We stopped redesigning and repaired single residues instead.

Fewer histidines, stronger grip

The engine was allowed up to four histidines per design, on the assumption that more charge means a bigger pH response. Each one is also a mutation at a binding interface. Re-running the identical six designs with the cap lowered to two settled it.

Every design improved. The top row is the control: it had only one histidine to begin with, so the lower cap could not touch it — and it returned the identical score, which rules out the gains being drift between runs. Three designs crossed the usability bar and went into the submission.

Why a histidine next to an arginine does anything

The placement rule came from measuring 557 contacts across 17 crystal structures, before any design was made.

Nature packs a histidine within 4 Å of a negative carboxylate 22.3% of the time, and within 4 Å of a positive arginine or lysine only 9.7% of the time — 2.3× rarer. Close His–cation packing is something proteins avoid. Put a histidine there deliberately and protonation has to cost something. The same engine, run in reverse on an antibody–FcRn complex it had never seen, recovered both histidines of that textbook pH switch and did not fire in the wrong direction.

The twenty

Ten switches, each paired with its own histidine-free twin. The pair is the experiment: a drop in binding at pH 6.0 only means something if the twin, two or three residues apart, does not show it.

Every switch here has an i_ptm reproduced across two independent runs, an epitope confirmed on its own predicted structure rather than its parent's, at least five contacts on the intended site, and histidines verified to still face a target cation after refolding. One design carries a histidine 10.2 Å from its partner — out of reach, and counted as such.
