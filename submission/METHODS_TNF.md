# Challenge 2 — acid-OFF pH-switchable binders against TNF-α

Track 3. Twenty designs: ten pH-switch candidates, each submitted with its own
histidine-free control. All designs are de novo, hallucinated from noise with no
template and no starting binder.

---

## 1. Summary

Designs were generated by AfDesign binder hallucination against a cropped TNF-α
interface, scored only by independent re-prediction, filtered on whether they
contacted the intended epitope, then given histidines positioned to clash with a
target arginine when protonated below pH 6.5. Every stage discarded work: 43
trajectories produced 10 submitted switches.

The ten switches are ranked by the challenge's own objective order — pH-selectivity
first, mouse cross-reactivity second, human affinity third. The ten controls follow,
each in the same order as its partner.

---

## 2. Target construct

- Structure: PDB 1TNF, chains A and B (two protomers of the trimer).
- Epitope: the receptor-binding site at the A/B protomer interface, the site
  occupied by TNFR2 and by adalimumab and infliximab. UniProt numbering throughout,
  offset +76 from 1TNF author numbering, verified by reproducing the challenge
  page's own 124/157 = 79.0% human/mouse identity figure.
- Crop: a 22 Å sphere centred between K166 (chain A) and R108 (chain B), giving a
  122-residue construct (68 from A, 54 from B).

The crop was a cost decision that turned out to matter for quality. A smoke test
measured 43.3 s per design iteration on the full 304+70 construct — cost scaling
closer to L^3.7 than L^2 — which would have bought about six designs with the
available compute. The 122-residue crop ran at roughly 26 min per complete design
and bought 43.

Two properties of the crop were checked rather than assumed, as assertions inside
every run: all 25 intended site residues are retained, and both conserved cationic
anchors (K166 chain A, R108 chain B) are present. Measured against the intact
trimer, the crop introduces no artificially exposed surface at the site — fewer
artefacts than an uncropped two-chain construct, because cropping also removes
residues that chain C had been covering.

## 3. Generation

ColabDesign AfDesign, `protocol="binder"`, three-stage optimisation
(90 soft / 40 temp / 20 hard), binder length sampled uniformly from 55–80 aa,
hotspots specified at the A/B interface. Two rounds with different random seeds:
27 designs and 16 designs, 9.6 GPU-hours total on two T4s.

**Every design is scored by an independent re-prediction of its discrete sequence at
3 recycles, never by the optimiser's own reported number.** This discipline came
from Challenge 1, where a design reporting i_ptm 0.725 during optimisation scored
0.100 on honest re-prediction. On this target the correction mostly runs the other
way — re-prediction raised the score for most good designs — but the direction is
not a property of the method, and the two numbers correlate inconsistently across
rounds (r = +0.906 in round 1, +0.753 in round 2), which is why the optimiser's
score is not used for ranking.

**Each design also reports which target residues it actually contacts**, in UniProt
numbering, mapped through an explicit construct map rather than through assumed
residue numbering. In Challenge 1 the designs drifted off the intended patch and
this was discovered only at the end of the campaign. Here it is measured inline.

That check earned its cost. One round-2 design scored i_ptm 0.667, second best of
sixteen, and contacted 27 residues of TNF-α — none of them among the 25 target site
residues. On score alone it would have entered this submission.

## 4. The acid-OFF switch rule

The requirement inverts the usual pH switch. The common engineered case binds in
acid and releases at neutral pH, using histidine against a target carboxylate. Here
the design must hold at pH 7.4 and release at pH 6.0, so the histidine is placed
facing a target **cation** — an arginine or lysine. Neutral imidazole at 7.4 is
tolerated beside it; imidazolium at 6.0 is not.

Histidine is the only residue whose side chain titrates across this range: free
imidazole has pKa ≈ 6.0, so it is about 3.8% charged at pH 7.4 and 50% charged at
pH 6.0. No other residue changes charge over that span.

### Calibration

The placement rule was calibrated before any design was made, on 17 crystal
structures, measuring 278 histidine-to-carboxylate and 279 histidine-to-cation
closest approaches:

| contact | 5th percentile | median | within 4.0 Å |
|---|---|---|---|
| His → carboxylate | 2.81 Å | 5.93 Å | 22.3% |
| His → Arg / Lys | 3.57 Å | 7.74 Å | 9.7% |

Two results followed. Close His–cation packing is 2.3× rarer in nature than
His–carboxylate packing, which is the signal the acid-OFF switch exploits: placing a
histidine where proteins normally avoid one means protonation costs something.
Second, and unexpectedly, the Cβ distance distributions of the two cases are nearly
identical (medians 6.54 and 6.39 Å) — the *geometry* of contact does not distinguish
them, only the *frequency* does. The distance window therefore transfers between
switch directions, and only the partner class and the scoring shape change.

Scoring shapes differ because the physics differs. An attractive salt bridge has an
optimum separation, so acid-ON quality peaks near the median contact distance.
Repulsion rises monotonically as charges approach, so acid-OFF quality increases as
the contact tightens, weighted by burial because solvent screens charge.

Accepted window: Cβ-to-cation-nitrogen 3.0–8.8 Å, with an orientation filter
(cos ≥ −0.30 between the Cα→Cβ vector and the vector to the partner). Positions that
are already Arg, Lys or His are excluded: substituting a cation for histidine would
keep a positive charge at pH 6.0 and lose one at 7.4, which is the wrong direction.

### External validation

The rule was tested on a system with independently known pH behaviour that was never
used to build it: PDB 4N0U, human FcRn bound to an IgG Fc. That is the textbook
acid-ON switch, mediated by Fc His310 and His435. Run in acid-ON mode the rule
recovered both residues among its candidates, ranking them 3rd and 9th of 10 by
geometry alone. Run in acid-OFF mode on the same complex it identified neither. The
inversion is therefore not cosmetic.

This validation also corrected the Challenge 1 implementation. Its window of
3.5–7.0 Å had been set by eye and would have missed both known FcRn switch residues
— His310 at 3.09 Å and His435 at 7.46 Å — so the window was widened to the measured
percentiles.

### Partner selection

Phase 1 identified two conserved, exposed cationic anchors in the site: K166 (A) and
R108 (B). The implementation does not restrict placement to them. Applying the same
exposure rule across the whole crop finds six exposed Arg/Lys residues, four of them
inside the site. In Challenge 1 an over-narrow whitelist was the limiting factor on
switch placement, and widening it was what produced working switches.

Widening did not cost precision: the two cations lying outside the site were never
selected, excluded by the distance and orientation filters alone.

It did, however, introduce a species liability, described in section 7.

## 5. Sequence liabilities

ProteinMPNN redesign was tested in Challenge 1 and rejected on evidence: it raised
fold confidence (pLDDT 0.82–0.87) while destroying the interface on 12 of 14
backbones, taking i_ptm from 0.50–0.79 down to about 0.10.

Instead, liabilities are repaired residue by residue: free cysteines to serine, and
homopolymer runs of 5 or more broken with conservative same-class substitutions.
Designed histidines are protected from repair. Every repaired sequence is
re-predicted, so the cost of each repair is measured.

Across 19 controls, repair was close to free — i_ptm retention 88–111%, median 100%,
with several improving. One design was an exception at 69% retention, where five
substitutions including three leucine-to-isoleucine changes inside a poly-leucine run
were enough to damage the fold. That design is not in this submission.

All 20 submitted sequences are free of cysteines and contain no homopolymer run
longer than 4.

## 6. Confirmation

Switch variants were initially scored without saving their predicted complexes,
which left two things assumed rather than measured. Both were then measured in a
dedicated run that re-predicted every candidate with structures retained.

**Does the switch still bind the same epitope?** Retaining i_ptm does not establish
this; a histidine could shift the binding mode and keep a similar score. Measured on
each switch's own predicted structure, 11 of 12 candidates retained at least 60% of
the parent epitope together with 5 or more contacts on the site. The one failure
retained 68% where its own control retained 92%, and makes 4 site contacts against
its control's 9. The matched control is what makes that attributable to the
histidines rather than to prediction variance. It was dropped.

**Do the histidines still face their partners after refolding?** Placement is
computed on the parent backbone; whether the geometry survives refolding with two or
three residues changed is a separate question, and it is the one the entire mechanism
rests on. Across the submitted designs, every histidine but one remains within the
8.8 Å window, many at 3.1–5.8 Å — closer than the 7.74 Å median for natural
His–cation contacts, which is the regime where protonation should cost energy. One
design carries a third histidine at 10.2 Å, out of reach and counted as
non-functional in its ranking.

This run also established that **i_ptm is reproducible**: across independent
re-predictions of the same sequences, maximum deviation 0.026, median 0.001, 11 of 12
within 0.005.

## 7. Two findings from the campaign

### Fewer histidines retain more binding

The engine was initially allowed up to four histidines per design, on the assumption
that more titratable charge means a larger pH response. Each one is also a mutation
at a binding interface. Re-running six identical designs with the cap lowered from
four to two settled it:

| design | cap 4 | cap 2 | Δ i_ptm |
|---|---|---|---|
| tnf2_w0_008 | 1 His · 0.598 | 1 His · 0.598 | +0.000 |
| tnf2_w1_001 | 3 His · 0.443 | 2 His · 0.553 | +0.110 |
| tnf2_w0_007 | 3 His · 0.414 | 2 His · 0.487 | +0.073 |
| tnf2_w0_001 | 3 His · 0.418 | 2 His · 0.470 | +0.052 |
| tnf2_w0_006 | 3 His · 0.286 | 2 His · 0.444 | +0.158 |
| tnf2_w1_003 | 4 His · 0.236 | 2 His · 0.253 | +0.017 |

Every design improved. The first row is a built-in control: it had only one
histidine to begin with, so the lower cap could not change it, and it returned the
identical score — which rules out the gains being drift between runs. Three designs
crossed the usability threshold and are in this submission.

### R107 is not conserved, and this changes the ranking

The site is 76% identical between human and mouse (19 of 25 residues). The
substitutions are N106S, R107Q, I159F, V161I, T165E and R207Q.

**R107 is glutamine in mouse.** A histidine placed against R107 therefore has no
cationic partner in the mouse ortholog, and that design's switch mechanism is
human-specific. Both conserved anchors chosen in Phase 1 — K166 and R108 — are
identical in mouse, which is why they were chosen.

Two otherwise strong designs place every histidine on R107. One of them,
`tnf_sw_04`, has the strongest human switch geometry of all candidates and no mouse
switch at all. They are included, ranked on their human pH-selectivity as the
objective order requires, and flagged in the metrics table so the distinction is
visible rather than buried.

Note the assay design: pH-selectivity is measured against human TNF-α at both pH
values, while mouse binding is measured at pH 7.4 only. A human-only switch
therefore costs nothing against objectives 1 and 2 taken separately. It matters for
a design judged on both together.

## 8. Ranking method

Objective order from the challenge page: pH-selective binding, then mouse
cross-reactivity, then human affinity. Designs are ranked accordingly:

1. **Switch strength** — the sum over functional histidines of
   `(8.8 − d) / (8.8 − 3.0)`, where d is the measured Cβ-to-cation distance in the
   confirmed structure. Histidines outside the 8.8 Å window contribute zero. This is
   banded rather than sorted continuously (≥1.2, 0.6–1.2, <0.6), because differences
   within a band are smaller than the confidence in the measurement behind them.
2. **Epitope conservation in mouse** — the fraction of each design's confirmed
   epitope that is identical between human and mouse TNF-α.
3. **Predicted affinity** — re-predicted AlphaFold2 i_ptm.

The ten controls follow the ten switches, each in its partner's position.

---

## 9. Why controls are submitted

Each switch is paired with its own sequence minus the histidines, differing by one to
three residues and otherwise identical, including the same liability repairs. A drop
in binding at pH 6.0 is only interpretable against that twin. Without it, a
pH-dependent change could come from the fold, the repair, or anything else about the
sequence. With it, the comparison isolates the designed mechanism.

The controls are not expected to be pH-selective and are not presented as switch
candidates.

## 10. Limitations

Stated plainly, because they bear on how much the predicted numbers are worth.

- **No model used here represents pH or protonation state.** AlphaFold2 has no
  concept of either, so i_ptm cannot report what happens at pH 6.0. Every predicted
  number in this submission describes the neutral-pH complex. The pH response is a
  structural hypothesis grounded in the calibration and the FcRn control, not a
  computed result.
- **i_ptm is a predicted score, not an affinity.** It is used for ranking within this
  set, not as a claim about binding constants.
- **Structural novelty is asserted from the method**, not measured. Backbones are
  hallucinated from noise with no template and no starting binder, which makes them
  de novo by construction, but no structural similarity search against the PDB was
  run.
- **Mouse binding was not modelled.** The mouse assessment is sequence conservation
  across each design's confirmed human epitope. No design was folded against mouse
  TNF-α.
- **Single-conformation analysis.** All geometry comes from one predicted structure
  per sequence, with no sampling of side-chain or backbone flexibility, so the
  histidine-to-cation distances carry no error estimate.
- **The target was a two-protomer crop**, while the assay uses the soluble trimer.
  The crop was checked against the intact trimer for artificially exposed surface at
  the site and showed none, but an interface relying on surface that exists only
  because chain C is absent would not reproduce.
